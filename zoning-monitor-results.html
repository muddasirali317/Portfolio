Case study · Zoning automation · United States

Knowing which cities changed their zoning.

Cities update their online zoning maps without announcing it. This Python system checks each city's ArcGIS layer on a schedule, filters out republishing noise, and reports exactly which cities changed and what changed, with a map to review every change.

01 · Method

How it works

Most checks are cheap. A full download and comparison only happens when something looks different, plus a staggered safety rotation, so large city lists stay fast and polite to city servers.

STEP 01

Cheap signals first

Edit dates, feature counts, extent and schema are read from each layer. Unchanged signals end the check quickly without downloading the data.

STEP 02

Compare, ignore noise

When signals differ, the layer is downloaded and normalized. Reordered features, renumbered IDs, split polygons, rounding and reprojection are filtered out as noise.

STEP 03

Classify and rank

Real changes are classified (boundary, codes added, removed or renamed, attributes, schema, extent, broken source), drawn as change polygons and scored by priority.

02 · Review

Interactive map viewer

Reviewers open any city to see its current zoning coloured by zone code, the previous baseline, and every detected change. Layers, zone codes and opacity can be switched individually.

03 · Operations

Monitoring and issues

Every city ends each run with exactly one status. The dashboard summarises the run, and the Issues page groups problem cities by cause with the steps to fix each one.

Stress-tested on 500 simulated cities (213,000 features) across 12 edit and server-failure scenarios: every city classified correctly
